Privacy policy

Effective November 17, 2026

Ingletide is an app that keeps a record of the time you spend with the people you love, with your phones set aside. This policy explains what information the app collects, why, who can see it, and the choices you have. Ingletide is run by an independent developer in British Columbia, Canada, and follows Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA).

The short version: we collect only what the app needs to work. We don't sell your information, we don't show ads, we don't track your location, and you can delete your account and everything in it at any time.

The detailed log of each session (every lock, unlock and nearby phone) stays on your phone. Once everyone's phones have confirmed the session's totals, the log is deleted from your phone too.

We don't collect your GPS location, your contacts, your messages, other photos on your phone, or anything you do in other apps. Ingletide asks for Bluetooth ("Nearby devices" on Android) and notification permissions. It uses them only to find other Ingletide phones and to show the together time timer and join prompts.

Who can see what

  • Your People see your name, your photo, and the time you've spent together. They never see your time with anyone else.
  • People in the same session who aren't yet your People see only your first name, so they know who was there.
  • Moments are visible to the people who were in that session.
  • Nicknames are visible only to you.

Nothing is ranked or compared between people, and nothing is public.

How we use your information

  • To run the app: your account, your People and your time together.
  • To keep the app secure and working, and to fix bugs.
  • To answer you when you contact us.

We don't sell or rent your information, and we don't use it for advertising.

Who we share it with

We use a small number of service providers to run the app. They process data only on our behalf:

  • Supabase hosts our database, photos and sign-in system.
  • Google provides Sign in with Google, app distribution through Google Play, and notifications on Android (Firebase Cloud Messaging).
  • Apple provides Sign in with Apple, app distribution through the App Store, and notifications on iPhone.
  • Expo delivers app updates to your phone.

Join notifications carry no names or personal details; your phone fills those in itself. These providers may store data on servers outside Canada, including in the United States, where it may be subject to local laws. We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

How long we keep it

We keep your information while your account is open. When you delete your account in the app, your account, photos and records are deleted straight away. If you ask us by email, we do it within 30 days. Copies in our backups are cleared within a further 30 days.

The people you spent time with keep their own records with each other, but your name, photo and moments are removed.

Your choices

  • See or correct your information. Email us and we'll send you a copy or fix anything that's wrong.
  • Change or remove your photo at any time in Settings.
  • Edit or delete your moments on the Moments tab.
  • Delete your account in the app or by email. See Delete your account.
  • Turn off Bluetooth or notification permissions in your phone's settings. Without Bluetooth, together time can't be counted.

Children

Ingletide accounts are for people 13 and older. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, contact us and we'll delete it.

Security

Your information is encrypted while it travels between your phone and our servers. Database rules let each person read only what they're allowed to see, as described above. No system is perfectly secure, but we take reasonable steps to protect your data.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top and, for significant changes, let you know in the app.

Contact

Questions or requests about your privacy: support@ingletide.com.

If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
